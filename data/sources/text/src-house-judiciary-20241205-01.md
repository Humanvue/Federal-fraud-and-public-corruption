Dear Mr. Estrada:  
 
On October 19, 2021, the U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Central District of California 
indicted then-United States Representative Jeff Fortenberry for allegedly making materially false 
statements to federal agents.1 A California federal jury found Representative Fortenberry guilty 
on these charges,2 and the court sentenced him to two years of probation.3 On December 26, 
2023, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit reversed the conviction, holding that 
Representative “Fortenberry’s trial took place in a state where no charged crime was committed 
. . . .”4  
 
There are serious concerns about the Department’s abusive tactics in its prosecution of 
Representative Fortenberry.5 Among other concerns, Representative Fortenberry’s former 
attorney accused the lead prosecutor of misleading him about Representative Fortenberry’s status 
as a witness, subject, or target of the investigation and about the nature of the Department’s 
investigation.6 Despite the Ninth Circuit reversing Representative Fortenberry’s conviction, on 
May 8, 2024, the Department has indicted him again for much of the same alleged conduct in 
Washington, D.C.7 As the Committee continues to examine the politicization of the Department 
of Justice, we ask that you preserve all records concerning the prosecution of Representative 
Fortenberry in the Central District of California.

This letter serves as a formal request to preserve all existing and future records and 
materials related to your office’s investigation and prosecution of Representative Fortenberry. 
You should construe this preservation notice as an instruction to take all reasonable steps to 
prevent the destruction or alteration, whether intentionally or negligently, of all documents, 
communications, and other information, including electronic information and metadata, that are 
or may be responsive to this congressional inquiry. This instruction includes all electronic 
messages sent using official and personal accounts or devices, including records created using 
text messages, phone-based message applications, or encryption software.  
 
If you have any questions about these requests, please contact Committee staff at (202) 
225-6906. Pursuant to the Rules of House of Representatives, the Committee on the Judiciary is 
authorized to conduct oversight of the Justice Department.8  
 
Thank you for your prompt attention to this matter. 
 
Sincerely, 
 
 
 
Jim Jordan 
Chairman
